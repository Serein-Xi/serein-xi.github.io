# Quantum Field Theory Notes

A concise collection of physical interpretations and mathematical tools for introductory quantum field theory.

## Why Quantum Fields?

Relativistic quantum theories allow particle number to change. A field formulation naturally accommodates particle creation and annihilation while maintaining locality and Lorentz symmetry.

## Propagators

A propagator describes how a field disturbance contributes to an amplitude between spacetime points. Different boundary conditions lead to different propagators, such as retarded and Feynman propagators.

## Perturbation Theory

Interactions are organized in powers of coupling constants. Tree-level diagrams provide the lowest-order contribution; loop diagrams account for higher-order quantum fluctuations and radiative corrections.

## Renormalization

Loop integrals can contain ultraviolet divergences. Renormalization defines how bare parameters and fields relate to finite, measurable quantities at a chosen scale or within a chosen scheme.

The physical lesson is that measured masses and couplings include quantum effects; bare Lagrangian parameters are not directly observables.

## Infrared Behavior

In theories with massless particles, sufficiently soft or collinear emissions can produce infrared singularities in intermediate calculations. Suitable inclusive observables combine real and virtual corrections so that physically measurable predictions remain meaningful.

## Unitarity and the Optical Theorem

Conservation of total probability constrains scattering amplitudes. The optical theorem relates the imaginary part of forward scattering to sums over allowed intermediate final states.
